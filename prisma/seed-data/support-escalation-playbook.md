# Support Escalation Playbook

Version 1.9 · Owner: Customer Operations · Last reviewed: 5 March

## 1. Priority definitions

- **URGENT** — money is missing, an order is lost, or the customer is blocked from using a
  paid product. First response within 1 hour, resolution target 8 hours.
- **HIGH** — delivery delay beyond SLA, hardware failure, repeated contact on the same issue.
  First response within 4 hours, resolution target 2 working days.
- **MEDIUM** — general product questions, non-blocking account problems. First response within
  1 working day.
- **LOW** — feedback, feature requests, informational queries. Best effort.

## 2. When to escalate to a human specialist

Escalate immediately, without attempting a resolution, when:

- the customer mentions legal action, a regulator, or the consumer court,
- the customer asks explicitly to speak to a person,
- the request involves fraud, chargebacks or suspected account takeover,
- a refund above ₹25,000 is being requested,
- the customer has contacted us three or more times about the same unresolved issue,
- the answer is not covered by any policy document.

Escalation is not a failure. Handing a case over early with a clear summary is better than a
confident wrong answer.

## 3. What an escalation must contain

An escalation summary must state the customer, the order or ticket reference, what has been
tried, what the customer wants, and what decision is needed from the human. Never escalate
with only "customer is unhappy".

## 4. Tone

Acknowledge the problem in the first sentence. Do not open with an apology template. Never
speculate about the cause of a failure, and never blame the carrier, the customer or another
team by name.

## 5. Commitments an operator may not make

- A specific delivery date that the carrier has not confirmed.
- Compensation above ₹500 without a team lead's approval.
- A refund on an order that has not been paid for.
- Any promise about a future product release date.

## 6. After-hours cover

Outside 09:00–21:00 IST, only `URGENT` tickets are actioned. Everything else is queued for the
next working day, and the customer is told when to expect a reply.
